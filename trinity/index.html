Trinity School Croydon 11+ entrance: how to prepare

Independent boys' school, CroydonSeptember 2027 entry

Trinity School is an independent boys' school in Croydon. At 11+, boys take an on-screen assessment made by Quest, a short written paper and an academic interview.

This page explains each part in plain English, with the timings of each section, what is tested and a simple plan for preparing. It is written in our own words from Trinity School's and Quest's published guidance, with links to their pages.

At a glance

An on-screen test made by Quest, a separate written paper, and an academic interview.

What is tested

  • A computer based assessment from Quest lasting 70 minutes: English (20 minutes), Verbal reasoning (10), Maths (30) and Non verbal reasoning (10).
  • Most questions are multiple choice. A few Maths questions ask you to type the answer.
  • English is not adaptive: everyone sees the same questions and can go back to check. The other sections adapt to how well your child is doing and have no Back button.
  • A separate written paper of about 30 minutes, where children write a personal response.
  • Spelling and vocabulary questions draw on words from the official Quest vocabulary list.

After the test

  • An academic interview that covers your child's interests and some lateral thinking questions.
  • Quest's own guidance says children do not need special preparation for the interview. Being themselves is what counts.
  • Scholarship assessments run separately. See the scholarships page for details.

How to prepare: a simple plan

  1. Try the free familiarisation testQuest offers a free taster for Trinity. Doing it together means the screen layout and buttons are familiar on the day.
  2. Practise without a Back buttonMost sections adapt to your child's answers and do not let them go back. Practise reading each question carefully and committing to an answer.
  3. Learn the Quest vocabulary listSpelling and vocabulary questions draw on Quest's published word list. A few words a day adds up quickly.
  4. Read widelyQuest publishes a reading list. Regular reading helps the English section and the written paper.
  5. Write a personal responseThe written paper asks for a personal response in about 30 minutes. Practise planning for a few minutes, then writing clearly.

Tips from the school's guidance

  • Do the free Quest familiarisation test together, so the screen layout and buttons are not a surprise on the day.
  • Read widely and often. Quest publishes a reading list of suggested books.
  • Learn a few words a day from the Quest vocabulary list. The spelling questions here use that list too.
  • Practise thinking out loud about a puzzle: interviewers like to hear how a child reaches an answer, not just the answer.

Questions parents ask

How long is the Trinity School 11+ assessment?

The on-screen Quest assessment lasts 70 minutes: English 20 minutes, verbal reasoning 10, Maths 30 and non verbal reasoning 10. There is also a separate written paper of about 30 minutes.

Is the Trinity test adaptive?

The English section is not adaptive, so everyone sees the same questions and can go back. The other sections adapt to how well your child is doing and have no Back button.

Does my child need to prepare for the Trinity interview?

Quest's guidance says children do not need special preparation for the interview. It covers their interests and some lateral thinking questions.
