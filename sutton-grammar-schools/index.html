Sutton grammar schools 11+ (the SET): how to prepare

Sutton Grammar, Wilson's, Wallington County Grammar, Wallington High School for Girls and Nonsuch High School for GirlsNext cycle: September 2028 entry

Five selective state schools in the London Borough of Sutton share one first test, the Selective Eligibility Test (SET): Sutton Grammar School, Wilson's School, Wallington County Grammar School, Wallington High School for Girls and Nonsuch High School for Girls.

Children who reach a school's pass mark in the SET go on to that school's second stage test. This page explains both stages in plain English, with the usual timetable and a simple plan for preparing. It is written in our own words from the schools' websites, with links to their own pages.

At a glance

One shared test (the SET) in September of Year 6, then a second stage test for children who reach a school's pass mark. The tests for 2027 entry have already happened, so the dates below are for planning the next round.

What is tested

  • The SET has two multiple choice papers, English and Maths, each about 40 to 50 minutes. There is no verbal or non verbal reasoning.
  • Answers are A to E, shaded on a separate answer sheet. There is no negative marking.
  • In English, some questions ask for two answers. Both must be right to get the mark.
  • The content is broadly the Year 5 and 6 primary curriculum.

After the test

  • Each school sets its own pass mark. Passing the SET only means a child can sit that school's second stage.
  • Boys: Sutton Grammar, Wallington County Grammar and Wilson's share a second stage. Wilson's describes it as written English and Maths papers, not multiple choice, and a child must pass both.
  • Girls: Nonsuch and Wallington High School for Girls run a joint second stage test (the NWSSEE).
  • Wilson's also offers optional music and football aptitude places for boys who pass the SET.
  • Passing the tests does not guarantee a place. You must still list the school on your council's Common Application Form.

How to prepare: a simple plan

  1. Print the free sample papersThe schools publish SET sample questions in English and Maths, plus a sample answer sheet. Use them early to see the style.
  2. Practise shading the answer sheetAnswers are A to E on a separate sheet. Practise shading neatly and rubbing out changes completely.
  3. Cover the Year 5 and 6 curriculumThe SET is broadly based on the primary curriculum. Short, regular practice on each Maths and English topic is the best preparation.
  4. Never leave a blankThere is no negative marking, so always choose an answer, even if unsure.
  5. Get ready for the second stageThe second stage is not multiple choice. Practise writing full answers and showing Maths working.

Tips from the school's guidance

  • Print the free sample questions and answer sheet and practise shading the lozenges neatly. Rub out changes completely.
  • No rough paper is given, so get used to working in the spare space on the question paper.
  • Never leave a multiple choice answer blank, as wrong answers do not lose marks.
  • For the second stage, practise writing full answers and showing Maths working, because it is not multiple choice.
  • Nonsuch and Wallington Girls both say tutoring is not necessary, as the test is based on the primary curriculum.
  • Some grammar schools keep a number of places for children who qualify for the Pupil Premium. Read the school's admissions policy to see whether it does and how to show your child qualifies.
  • On the day bring two HB or B pencils, a soft rubber, a sharpener and a ruler in a clear case. Phones, calculators and smartwatches are not allowed; only analogue watches.

Questions parents ask

Which schools use the Sutton SET?

Sutton Grammar School, Wilson's School, Wallington County Grammar School, Wallington High School for Girls and Nonsuch High School for Girls.

Is there verbal or non verbal reasoning in the SET?

No. The SET has two multiple choice papers, English and Maths, each about 40 to 50 minutes.

Does passing the SET guarantee a place?

No. Passing the SET only means your child can sit that school's second stage, and you must still list the school on your council's application form.
